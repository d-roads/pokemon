The numbers, explained.

Market price & buy target

Prices use the narrowest 30-, 90-, or 180-day window with at least 3 matching sales. Smaller samples are shown with a limited-sample label. A buy target also requires a sale in the last 90 days. By default, the target is 15% below that median. You can choose your own target on a watched card.

PSA 9 and PSA 10 sales are separate. Raw targets use only listings explicitly identified as near mint. Mixed-condition raw guides and mixed-grader Grade 9 guides are shown as reference estimates, and are never used on their own to calculate a buy target.

Where the sales come from

Each rare card's PriceCharting page was read in full: the ungraded, Grade 9 and PSA 10 sold-listing tables, the monthly price history, and PSA population counts. A sale counts only when the table it was listed under agrees with the grade in its title, the card number matches, and it is not a lot, proxy, foreign-language copy, reverse holo, stamped or other variant. Every sale keeps its title and a link to the original listing.

Activity score / 100

This is a sales-activity measure for this grade, not a popularity or investment rating. It combines comparable sales in the last 90 days (7 points per sale, capped at 70) and recency (up to 30 points). Raw demand uses near-mint sales. Sparse coverage can understate demand. “—” means no observations.

Investment score / 100

A rating of how good a buy each card looks today in the grade you are viewing, built only from this app's sales, monthly guide history and catalog. It blends parts, each scored 0–100: character demand (30% raw / 27% graded, how far above similar cards that character's cards sell, the same measure the Investments tab uses), price momentum (25% / 22%, the trailing-year sold-price trend, given full credit only when it is statistically clear; rises above 150% a year are treated as too fast to trust), value (20% / 18%, price against same-set, same-rarity cards of no more popular characters, and distance below a high the monthly guide held for several months; a card that is still falling earns less credit for being low), liquidity (15% / 13%, the activity score), price stability (10% / 8%, how tightly recent sales cluster) and, for PSA 9 and PSA 10 only, grade scarcity (12%, how rarely PSA-graded copies reach the grade: the share that are PSA 10, or that reached PSA 9 or better, ranked against cards of the same era because gem rates differ widely between eras; needs 30+ graded copies). A part that can't be measured counts as average and is labeled. A card needs a confident sold price (the same evidence as a buy target) and a source check within the last 14 days to be scored, and cards under $25 are capped at 59. 80+ is Strong, 65–79 Good, 50–64 Fair, 35–49 Weak, under 35 Poor. It describes past sales, not a forecast or investment advice.

Research rank (shadow) and price check

A new system is being evaluated next to the investment score, and both are shown so they can be compared. The research rank is a 0–100 percentile among cards of the same grade, ranked on the last 13 completed months of prices: 75% on how far the price sits below its 13-month median and 25% on its momentum over the 11 months before the newest one. These weights were chosen on 2022 data and tested on 2024–2025 entries, where they beat an equal-weight basket of all cards on average but failed for Grade 9, Sword & Shield, Scarlet & Violet and several sets. It is a ranking of past prices, not a chance of profit. Raw cards are ranked on the ungraded guide (mixed condition) and PSA 9 on the Grade 9 guide (PSA and BGS mixed), so neither counts as near-mint or PSA-specific evidence. Mega Evolution and sets under a year old are not ranked.

Evidence is Supported only when the rank uses grade-matched history and the card has 8+ matching sale days in 180 days, one in the last 30, across 6 of the last 12 months, with fresh source data. Otherwise it says what is missing. The price check shows the most you could pay and still break even reselling at today's sold median after your fees, shipping and tax (default: 15% fees, $5 each way, no tax; change them under Cost assumptions). It is arithmetic on today's price, not a forecast. A 12-month forecast model was trained and tested too; it did not pass its validation checks (an unchanged price forecast better on held-out data), so no forecast is shown.

Filters

Price filters use the current reference price for the grade you are viewing. Activity and investment score filters leave out cards without a score. Era and set filters choose which cards are screened.

Your Dex

Each card you own is valued at the current reference for its grade: the sold median when there are recent matching sales, otherwise the source guide. Profit and loss compares that value with what you paid. The value-over-time chart uses the source's monthly price history for each grade; PSA 9 history is PriceCharting's Grade 9 guide, which includes other graders, so treat older months as an approximation. Selling fees, shipping and tax are not included.

Listing alerts

With your own eBay developer keys, FutureSight searches newly listed Buy It Now and Best Offer listings for each watched card and grade, and alerts when the price plus listed shipping is at or below your buy limit (or the suggested target, if you allow it). Listings must match the card number, the grade and the listing exclusions above. Each listing alerts once. Alerts run while the app is open on your computer.

Top movers

A move compares the median of matching sales in the last 7 days (week) or 30 days (month) with the median of the 4 weeks or 2 months before. A card is listed only when it was worth at least $25 at the start, has at least 3 matching sales on 2 or more days in both periods, most of the new sales are above the starting price, the sale prices are not too scattered, and the new median broadly agrees with the source's own guide. Jumps above 150% need at least 5 sales in each period. Raw moves use near-mint sales only. Cards that fail any check are left out, so a list can be shorter than 20.

Potential investments

Every pick needs a confident sold price of at least $25 (the same evidence a buy target needs) and must feature a character in the top quarter for collector demand. Character demand is measured from sales: how far above similar cards (same era and rarity) that character's cards sell, averaged across its cards and pulled toward average when there are only a few, plus how often its cards sell. A card then needs at least one signal. Steady uptrend: a clear rise of 15–150% a year across at least 8 sales over 6+ months in 3 or more quarters, with little scatter and recent sales confirming it. Recovering from highs: the monthly guide is at least 40% below a high it held for several months in the last five years, and sales in the last 45 days are at least 5% above the 90 days before. Cheap vs. similar cards: it sells for at most 65% of the median price of same-set, same-rarity cards of clearly less in-demand characters, compared with at least 4 of them; promos are not compared. At most 3 cards per character are listed. These are screens over past sales, not forecasts or investment advice.

Trend projection

The 1Y, 2Y and 3Y estimates fit a regression to daily sold medians from the trailing year. They need a confident sold price, eight sale dates spanning six months, a recent sale and a source check within 14 days. The last 30% of dates (at least three) are held out: the trend must have no more than 35% average percentage error and perform at least as well as unchanged prices. Extreme slopes are withheld. The historical test error is shown beside the chart; it does not validate multi-year returns. These are illustrative extrapolations, not investment advice.

Five-year scenarios

Bear, steady, and strong scenarios apply illustrative annual changes of −7%, +5%, and +12% to the current comparable median. They are assumptions, not forecasts or backtested predictions. They exclude selling fees, tax, and inflation.

Freshness

Price snapshots show their original observation date. Refresh attempts request the latest publicly available source data. If the source is unavailable or its format changes, saved observations keep their original date. A successful fetch does not make an old transaction recent. Movers expire after 7 days without a check; scores expire after 14 days. Public source-page snapshots are not complete eBay sales history.
